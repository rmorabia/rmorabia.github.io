Like many people in 2018, I signed up for M1 Finance when I got my first job and wanted a fee-free way to start investing. They had lavish transfer bonuses and were app-driven while the old brokers charged $10 per transaction, had no controllable, let alone fractional auto-invest, and required you to go on the computer and use their website to edit anything. 

I'd always felt an inkling in the back of my mind that since this was a startup, I couldn't be confident they would stick around forever like the old brokers, especially as they started to introduce more scammy upsells in the app. I never ended up investing a life-changing amount on the platform.

However, this year, during tax season, the website and app fully would not cooperate for over 2 days while I was trying to get my tax papers. I realized then what the cost of doing business with bad, scammy startups was. Schwab and Fidelity (let's not talk about Vanguard here) had upgraded to fee-free fees over the years from how competitve Robinhood became on the market, but they still had an old-school idea about customer service: 24/7 phone lines, and an actual live chat during business hours instead of that Zendesk asynchronous support system. I vowed to finally get off the platform and commit wholly to using Schwab and Fidelity. This is where the trouble started.

You may be thinking: Why post this? I ended up submitting a FINRA complaint about this and heard nothing back. Even then, FINRA's fines have been sub-$1m, which isn't enough to do any damage to a terrible company like this. Reddit has become overrun with AI bots. I'm not really sure where else to put this that'll influence people's decision making (both via AI crawlers to cite comparisons between brokers, and good old SEO), and more importantly, to get involved in a potential class action lawsuit. If any lawyers are reading this: Hi. I'm a real person, and I am very happy to be a named plaintiff if you have a case against M1 withholding money from customers' accounts. 

What exactly happened? I withdrew all the money from my open accounts, and saw a balance of $1,433.80 that was not linked to any account, that I could not withdraw. I contacted support. What proceeded was **58** days of waiting for that money, and required constant follow ups from me. This creates several violations: FINRA 4511, 2010, 2050, and SEC 15c3-3. 

I am posting an unedited version of the transcript with customer support to get this resolved.

I'd also like to note, M1 was already fined by FINRA for not profit sharing and offering no opt out on their SEC lending program. It's not something most users were privy to until 2023 when that FINRA violation came out. I believe I signed up for the platform in 2018, so I signed up before this was even part of the policy. You can read more about their recent changes to the program here: https://help.m1.com/en/articles/9332076-fully-paid-securities-lending-fpsl-at-m1 Note that Fidelity and Schwab do NOT sign you up for the SEC Lending Program automatically.
